# Beyond Fault Localization: A Trajectory-Level Study of LLM Agents for Microservice Root Cause Analysis — Highlights

Source: arXiv 2608.21310v2 (LaTeX in `source/arxiv-src/extracted/`, PDF in `source/arxiv-pdf/`). Section, table and figure numbers follow that version.

**Task.** Evaluate how LLM agents diagnose microservice incidents, not only whether they name the faulty service: given the traces, metrics and logs of an incident, an agent issues tool calls (mostly SQL over a telemetry store) over many rounds and reports a root-cause service plus a propagation graph; the paper asks what separates sound diagnostic processes from failing ones, why wrong diagnoses happen, and whether those failure patterns can guide an improvement (abstract; Section 1).

**Challenge.**
- Benchmarks score RCA by top-k localization (Acc@k, MRR, MAP), which collapses the diagnostic process into a final label; the same correct label can rest on strong or weak evidence, and a wrong label is only useful for improvement if its failed reasoning can be inspected (Section 1, Fig. 1).
- Raw trajectories cannot be graded directly: they interleave free-form reasoning with heterogeneous tool calls and telemetry results (abstract). Causal graphs in prior RCA work capture inferred service dependencies, not the route one incident's fault actually traveled, and agent-trace studies lack a domain ground truth to compare against (Section 1).
- RCABench provides root-cause labels but no process-level annotations, and heterogeneous agent frameworks expose different trace formats and tool interfaces (Section 1).

**Solution.** A trajectory-analysis methodology anchored on manually validated service-level fault propagation paths: each run is normalized into thought–action–result rounds and compared against the incident's propagation graph G* (Sections 4.1–4.2).
- Annotation: for 500 RCABench (TrainTicket) cases, the authors reconstruct G* from injection metadata, pre/post-injection telemetry and the static call graph; two reviewers agree on 97.4% of decisions (κ = 0.59) (Sections 4.1, 4.5).
- Outcome layer (RQ1): Acc@1, Node F1 and Edge F1 of the agent's reported graph against G*, cost (rounds, tokens, dollars), and accuracy by causal-chain depth d* (Section 4.2).
- Behavior layer (RQ2): each round gets a primary service; consecutive rounds get one of 10 navigation transitions (e.g. Reprobe, Stray, Wander, Retrace) relative to the fault-impact surface, and an evidence-utilization label (utilized, partial, ignored, no_data) for whether the next round acts on the on-path evidence the previous one returned; 92,501 SQL actions get one of 11 SRE intents (e.g. symptom_scan, trace_follow, resource_probe) plus six SQL-derived tags (Section 4.3, Table 1, Fig. 3).
- Failure layer (RQ3): two authors open-code all 154 failed ThinkDepth.ai trajectories against each fault type's expected evidence into 12 modes in three families: protocol omission (OMIT, evidence never queried), semantic misread (MIS, evidence retrieved but misread), general reasoning (GEN, failure untied to one evidence unit); 93.5% exact agreement (Sections 4.4–4.5, Table 3).
- Study: 5,000 trajectories from ten framework–backbone configurations on 500 RCABench cases: nine frameworks (ThinkDepth.ai, AIQ, TaskWeaver, ClaudeCode; OpenRCA, mABC, GoS, RCLAgent V2, OpsAgent) under Qwen 3.5-Plus, plus ThinkDepth.ai under Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Section 3).
- Intervention: DiagGuard wraps an unmodified framework (the Diagnostician) with a Verifier (a self-audit before investigation and a soundness check before committing, against OMIT/MIS and GEN) and a Grounder (a search-space profile of the actual data before the first query, against OMIT/MIS), added through prompts and tools with fixed rules (Section 6.1, Fig. 6a).

**Insight.** "Fault-propagation paths provide a causal reference for assessing where agents investigate and how they use evidence" (abstract): once each incident's path is known, every round of a trajectory can be placed on or off the fault-impact surface and every step judged by whether it used the evidence it surfaced. The failure taxonomy then "doubles as a design specification" for defenses (Section 6.1).

**Findings.**
- Correct localization hides weak causal reconstruction: even on correctly localized cases Edge F1 never exceeds 0.67, Edge F1 is below Node F1 in every configuration, and failed runs still find most anomalous services but no longer chain them (Section 5.1, Finding 2).
- Accuracy falls with causal-chain depth for nearly all Qwen frameworks (e.g. ClaudeCode 89.5% at depth 2 → 57.1% at depth 5), while Sonnet stays at 85.7–96.8% (Section 5.1, Table 2).
- More rounds do not buy better diagnoses: within a framework, failed runs use up to 11.4 more rounds than successful ones (Section 5.1, Finding 3).
- Successful runs stay on the fault-impact surface and act on what they retrieve (more Reprobe and utilized); failed runs Stray, Wander and Retrace and more often hit empty or ignored results; Sonnet acts on the prior result in 54.3% of rounds vs Qwen's 24.9% (Section 5.2, Finding 4). Qwen loops in shallow trace and log queries, while Sonnet pivots mid-trajectory to resource and topology queries (resource_probe in 82.8% vs 16.6% of trajectories) (Finding 5).
- Wrong diagnoses fall into three families: evidence never gathered, gathered but misread, or overridden by ungrounded reasoning; Qwen fails mostly by omission (e.g. OMIT3 resource gap 63.5%), Sonnet mostly by misreading (MIS3 86.0%) (Section 5.3, Finding 6). The modes recur on held-out RCA100, every mode is more prevalent in incorrect runs, and failure profiles are more similar within a framework across backbones (cosine 0.950) than across frameworks (0.767) (Section 6.2, Finding 8).

**Results.**
- RQ1 on RCABench (Acc@1): ThinkDepth.ai + Sonnet 90.0%; under Qwen the open-ended general-purpose agents lead (ClaudeCode 79.6%, ThinkDepth.ai 79.2%, AIQ 77.6%) and every RCA-specific framework trails (RCLAgent V2 53.4%, GoS 48.2%, OpenRCA 46.6%, OpsAgent 45.8%, mABC 42.6%); the backbone swap adds 10.8 pp, five times the 2.0 pp spread of the top band (Section 5.1, Finding 1, Table 2).
- DiagGuard raises Acc@1 in all 14 tested configurations: on AIOps 2025, ThinkDepth.ai +6.5 pp (Qwen) and +9.0 pp (Doubao); across 12 RCA100 configurations the median gain is 10.5 pp (range 3.5–35.1; mABC +21.1/+35.1 from baselines of 35.1% and 3.5%); Node F1 and Edge F1 improve in 11 of 12 RCA100 configurations (Section 6.2, Finding 7).
- Failure modes per run drop from 1.94 to 1.39 across 684 + 684 RCA100 runs, with significant reductions in seven modes (OMIT2 57.3% → 44.7%, GEN3 15.1% → 4.7%); MIS3 does not drop (11.8% → 13.6%) (Section 6.2).
- Ablation on AIOps 2025 (Doubao, 5 runs): baseline 43.5%, Grounder alone 48.0% (+4.5), Verifier alone 47.6% (+4.1), full DiagGuard 52.5% (+9.0), with pass@5 62.3% → 73.0%; the full stack costs $0.16 per incident vs $0.17 for Verifier alone (Section 6.3, Table 4).
